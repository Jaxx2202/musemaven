Formulator's Note

On the PM-only timing for Glass Skin Drops

Muse Maven's product page doesn't specify a time of day for this one. The PM placement above is a judgment call, not a stated direction: it's a 10% lactic acid (AHA) product, and AHAs generally increase sun sensitivity, so night use is the safer default. Worth confirming before this goes into customer-facing copy as an official recommendation.

On calling this a "retinol" routine

Glass Skin Drops sits in Muse Maven's Retinols collection, but by its own ingredient list it's a lactic acid and peptide serum, not a retinol. The only actual retinol in this bundle is inside The Perfect Derma Body Set lotion, and that's a body product, not a face treatment. Campaign copy should avoid promising "retinol for your face" anywhere this bundle is described.

On sun sensitivity

The Perfect Derma Body Set explicitly warns about AHA-related sun sensitivity and recommends SPF. Glass Skin Drops likely carries the same real-world caution, though her product page doesn't currently state it. Flagging as a possible gap to close on the product listing itself, separate from this bundle.

WHY THIS ORDER

For Black Friday 2026

The Sapphire Luxe Serum leads because it's the highest-value single item and the most visually distinctive, the blue tint photographs well and gives the bundle a clear hero shot for paid and organic content. The Oxygen Masque mid-bundle gives shoppers an immediate, low-effort payoff the same night they unbox, which is exactly the kind of proof point that drives repeat Black Friday buyers into January restocks.

For Mother's Day 2027

The same seven products regift cleanly. Reframe the copy around "a ritual, not a routine," lead with the Body Set and Turmeric Oil for their gift-set visual weight, and let the Sapphire Serum stay the hero image. Same inventory, same margin, a second full-price selling moment without building a new bundle from scratch.

GIFT WITH PURCHASE

She gets the bundle. You get something too.

Reframed for who's actually buying: this is a Mother's Day purchase, meaning the customer is almost always shopping for someone else's self-care. The gift with purchase flips that, it's a self-care moment for the person doing the gifting, scaled to what they spend on her.

Tier 1
Spend $100+ on her
$29.99 value, for you
e.g. Instant Oxygen Masque, real price $32.99, the closest single confirmed item, about $3 over this cap
Tier 2
Spend $200+ on her
$49.99 value, for you
e.g. Turmeric Body Oil, real price $42, one item, comfortably under this cap
Tier 3
Full Bundle · $349, on her
$69.99 value, for you
e.g. Pure Seed Oil, real price $39, one item, well under this cap, there's no single confirmed product between $42 and $89 on her site to fill the gap more closely

The pitch is simple: "You're taking care of her. Let us take care of you." Positions the gift-with-purchase as self-care for the shopper, not an extra present for Mom, which is a different emotional trigger than a standard GWP and should read stronger in ad copy.

Each gift is one physical item, priced at or under its tier's stated cap, not a bundled duo. Two real gaps this surfaced: no single confirmed product sits at or under $29.99 for Tier 1 (Instant Oxygen Masque is the closest, $32.99), and nothing sits between $42 and $89 for Tier 3, leaving that tier's real gift value well below its cap. You may want to introduce a smaller or mid-priced item to close that gap, or leave it as is.
