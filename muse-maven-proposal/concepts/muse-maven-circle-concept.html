EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Problem
Muse Maven already has three paid membership tiers built for retention, Luxury, Elegant, and Charming. What doesn't exist is a referral mechanism to bring new clients in. The two programs are currently unconnected.
Solution
A three-tier referral program paying account credit, valued against real membership prices, usable toward either a membership payment or a product purchase, the referrer's choice, rather than inventing a separate, unrelated reward system.
Why It Works
It doesn't ask clients to do anything they're not already doing, recommending Muse Maven to friends, and it gives the referral reward real weight by tying it to memberships clients already recognize and want.
Timeline
The fastest of the three proposed concepts to launch and execute. No content library required and no fixed launch date, so it moves as fast as approvals allow, 4 to 7 weeks from proposal approval.
Closes
Word of Mouth
HOW IT WORKS

Three tiers, and every referral rewards both people in it.

Enrollment starts automatically, no sign-up form standing between a client and the program. Every qualifying referral earns the referring client $50, every time, uncapped. Reaching 3 and 5 cumulative referrals also unlocks a one-time milestone bonus on top. The referred friend's welcome reward stays flat at every tier, and once they visit, they're enrolled too, so today's new client can be tomorrow's referrer.

How the credit actually adds up

The $50 referrer reward repeats every time a referral qualifies, up to a client's 10th rewarded referral in a calendar year, a cap that keeps balances from growing indefinitely. The $170 and $220 milestone bonuses each pay out once, when a referring client crosses 3 and 5 cumulative referrals. A client who refers 3 friends in a year earns $150 in base credit plus the $170 milestone bonus, $320 total, one of three referral tiers modeled in the 12-Month Accounting View below.

What the referred friend gets, and why the loop closes

Every referred friend gets the same $50 off their first visit, regardless of which tier sent them, and is automatically enrolled in Circle Access the moment that visit is complete. That means the reward program isn't just for existing clients, a friend who joins today has the same tiered path available to them tomorrow. One program, two entry points.

Why credit, not a free month

Muse Maven's membership contract runs on a fixed 6-month term with no unused-service carryover and a set monthly billing schedule. A "free month" doesn't map cleanly onto that structure, it would mean skipping a billed cycle mid-contract. A dollar-value account credit sidesteps this entirely: it works the same whether the referrer holds a membership or not, applies just as easily to product as to services, and never touches the contract's payment schedule. Same referral incentive, cleaner to administer.

Why this closes the gap

Word of mouth was flagged as a gap not because it isn't happening, but because nothing currently captures or rewards it, and it was never connected to the membership program already built to retain the clients it brings in. Muse Circle closes both gaps at once.

Future extension: medical wellness

Muse Maven also offers GLP-1 weight management and peptide therapy, services outside this proposal's current scope. Referral credit could eventually extend to those services too, a lighter-touch addition than Skin Story Series' extension, worth a second conversation once this program is running and that service line is in scope.

THE 12-MONTH ACCOUNTING VIEW

What this is worth in dollars, not just brand awareness.

Every tier's credit, base $50 per referral plus milestone bonuses, is redeemable toward a membership payment, a product order, or an à la carte service visit, the client's choice. Three caps keep it sustainable as balances grow: credit can cover at most 50% of any single transaction, base credit stops accruing after a client's 10th rewarded referral in a calendar year, and unredeemed credit expires 12 months after it's earned. What if a client splits credit across categories?

Nothing changes mechanically, the 50% per-transaction cap applies wherever the credit is spent. An Insider with $320 could put $85 toward two membership payments ($170 used), then apply the rest toward product, capped at $47.50 per order, using $142.50 across three orders, with the small remainder carrying forward. The three sections above show single-category use for clarity; in practice, most clients will likely mix, and the math is additive, whatever's redeemed in one category simply isn't available for another.

All nine scenarios use the same friend-side assumption, a $300 average first visit, $50 welcome credit, $250 net per referred friend, from the main proposal's ROI section. Product and service purchase frequency (4 orders, 3 visits per year) are estimates to confirm against real client behavior before these are treated as forecasts.
